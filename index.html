Paid Media — Full-Circle Automation
Autonomous Campaign Loop

The most ambitious project on the list — and the one with the highest long-term commercial payoff. A full-circle system that takes a campaign from idea to live ads to performance tracking to AI-optimised iteration, with human approval at each gate and no manual work in between.

● 6–10 weeks (staged build)
Pipeline impact
Very High (when live)
The six stages of the loop
  • Stage 1 — Ideation: You or Claude propose a campaign brief. Claude generates 5 concept variants with headline, hook, CTA, persona (CEO/HR leader), market (NO/SE/FI), and recommended format. You pick one or remix.
  • Stage 2 — Creative production: Approved concept feeds huma-content-engine for copy variants (5 per format: Meta feed/story, Google search/display). Simultaneously generates a Figma brief for the designer with visual direction and brand variables mapped.
  • Stage 3 — Review & approval: Slack message with copy + Figma link. Designer and writer approve or comment in-thread. No email, no chasing.
  • Stage 4 — Publishing: Approved assets pushed to Meta and Google Ads via API. Campaign structure, budget, targeting, and flight dates set from the original brief. Blocked pending API access from agency — unblockable independently.
  • Stage 5 — Performance monitoring: Weekly read-only pull of campaign metrics — impressions, CTR, CPC, conversions per variant. Routed to the Monday digest and stored in Notion.
  • Stage 6 — AI optimisation: Claude analyses performance data, identifies best and worst-performing variants, and proposes specific tweaks — headline change, new CTA, audience adjustment. Routed for approval before any change is made.
Build order — what's unblocked now

Stages 1–3 and 5 are buildable immediately. Stage 4 (publishing) and Stage 6 (optimisation loop) require ad account API access from the external agency managing Meta and Google Ads. Don't wait for that to start — begin now and the full loop snaps into place when access arrives.

  • Now (weeks 1–3): Stage 1 ideation, Stage 2 copy production + Figma brief, Stage 3 Slack approval flow
  • Now (weeks 3–4): Stage 5 read-only performance monitoring via reporting APIs — does not require publishing access
  • When API access arrives: Stage 4 publishing + Stage 6 optimisation loop slot in with no rebuild required

This is your 300K NOK/month ad spend operating with the intelligence of a 500K/month budget — because every krone is optimised continuously rather than monthly.

Content Distribution — Extension of current content pipeline
Blog → Social Automation Pipeline

Your 2026 social strategy has a non-negotiable rule: every blog post generates a LinkedIn carousel, a LinkedIn text post, and an Instagram concept. Right now that rule breaks every time there's a handoff. This project closes the loop — so every published blog automatically becomes a full social package, ready for your designer and writer to approve.

● Ready in 1–2 weeks
Pipeline impact
Medium-High
What it does — extends the current pipeline
  • Watches for new "Published" status in your Notion content pipeline database
  • Passes the published blog to huma-content-engine to extract the key argument, hook, and supporting evidence
  • Generates three social assets per blog: LinkedIn carousel brief (8–12 slides), LinkedIn text post (opinion angle), Instagram concept (Reel or carousel)
  • Routes a Slack brief to your designer with the carousel structure and your writer with the copy — both in one message, one click to approve
  • Once approved, pushes to the social content calendar in Notion, ready to schedule
Why it matters to 2026

Your social goals are aggressive: LinkedIn 50K impressions/month at 3–4 posts/week, Instagram 3×/week. The only way to hit those numbers sustainably — given a one-person writer and one designer — is automating the distribution layer. Your content is already being written. The bottleneck is the gap between "blog published" and "social posts scheduled." This project eliminates that gap entirely. It's directly additive to Pillar 3 (Content & Thought Leadership) and mirrors the blog → social pipeline rule already written into your 2026 social strategy.

Reporting & Ops
Weekly Marketing Intelligence Digest

Three hours per week disappear into pulling HubSpot data, checking content output, scanning pipeline movement, and assembling a status update no one asked for in slide form. The intelligence digest replaces all of that with one Slack message, every Monday morning, built automatically from live data — with both a weekly view and a rolling LTM lens so you're never just reacting.

● Ready in 1 week
Pipeline impact
Medium
Five fixed sections — with LTM anchoring
  • Funnel snapshot: Leads, MQLs, SQLs, wins this week vs last week vs LTM average vs 2026 OKR target — traffic light per metric. The LTM anchor prevents over-reacting to a quiet week or over-celebrating a lucky one.
  • Deal quality matrix: Win rates broken out by country (NO / SE / FI) AND by deal size bucket — under 50K NOK, 50–85K NOK, 85K+ NOK. Surfaces the critical question: are we winning the right deals, or filling the pipeline with large deals we can't close?
  • Content output: Posts published, blog articles live, social performance — open/click rates, top-performing piece this week vs LTM average.
  • Top 3 actions: Claude synthesises what needs to happen this week based on the data above — not generic, tied to actual numbers.
  • One decision needed: The single thing that requires a human call — flagged clearly, not buried in a wall of data.
Why the deal size matrix matters

The deal size × win rate × country breakdown is the most strategically important addition. Your 2026 plan sets a SQL → Win target of 40% for Norway and 25% for SE/FI — but those numbers hide a lot of nuance. If you're winning 60% of deals under 50K NOK and 10% of deals above 85K NOK, your SDR time and nurture resources are misallocated. If Norway is winning large deals but SE/FI are only converting small ones, the expansion thesis needs a rethink.

The LTM reference point serves a different purpose: it stops the digest from creating panic or false confidence on short runs. A single quiet week in August looks alarming without LTM context; a record week in October looks like a trend when it isn't. Both views — weekly and LTM — need to be visible simultaneously.

Brand & Content Strategy
Autonomous Competitive Positioning Agent

Simployer, Personio, HaileyHR, and Sympa update their pricing pages, add features, and run promotions continuously. And customers leave reviews on G2, Capterra, and Trustpilot that reveal gaps and frustrations your sales team could be exploiting in real time. Right now, none of that intelligence reaches the Huma team unless someone happens to spot it.

● Ready in 2–3 weeks
Pipeline impact
High
What it does
  • Page monitoring: Scrapes homepage, pricing, features, changelog, and blog weekly from Simployer, Personio, HaileyHR, and Sympa — diffs against the previous week and surfaces every change
  • Review site scraping: Pulls new reviews from G2, Capterra, and Trustpilot for each competitor — extracts recurring complaints, feature requests, and praise patterns. A complaint about Simployer's onboarding is a positioning opportunity for Huma's simplicity story.
  • Positioning brief: Claude synthesises page diffs + review themes into a weekly brief: what changed, what customers are saying, and a suggested messaging adjustment or battlecard update
  • Delivery: Routed to a dedicated Slack channel every Monday — same time as the intel digest, so both land together
  • Battlecard feed: Pushes updates directly into the SDR battlecard pages in Notion — no manual copy-paste required
Why it matters to 2026

Your 2026 plan explicitly includes SDR battlecards for Simployer, HaileyHR, Sympa, and 4Human. But battlecards go stale fast — especially when competitors are actively repositioning. An always-on agent means those cards stay current without anyone having to maintain them manually.

The review site layer is what makes this more than just a page-diff tool. Competitor reviews are a live feed of buyer frustrations — the specific objections, integration failures, and UX complaints that come up in sales calls. Knowing that Simployer customers are frustrated about mobile experience, or that HaileyHR is getting dinged for support response times, gives your SDRs a precise, credible counter-narrative.

In SE and FI — where win rates sit at 25% and you're still building credibility — sharp, current competitive positioning is a direct lever on the SQL → Win rate.

Demand Generation / SDR Enablement
ICP Signal Monitor → Warm Lead Alerts

Companies that are actively hiring HR roles, expanding headcount, or showing growth in the Nordic public business registries are already in-market for Huma — they just haven't raised their hand yet. Instead of waiting, this system watches for them and routes them directly to your qualifying SDR the moment they show intent — already enriched and ready to act on.

● Ready in 1–2 weeks
Pipeline impact
Very High
What it does
  • Job board monitoring: Watches Finn.no, LinkedIn, and Indeed SE/FI for open HR roles at ICP companies — a leading indicator they're actively building HR capacity and likely evaluating tools
  • Nordic business registries: Monitors Proff.no (Norway), Proff.se (Sweden), and Finder.fi (Finland) for filing activity, revenue growth, and headcount changes — public accounting and corporate data that signals company health and expansion
  • AI prospecting tool integration: Connects to the AI prospecting tool already in use by your SDRs and Account Executives — so signals flow directly into their existing workflow rather than creating a new inbox to check
  • HubSpot sync: Cross-references signals against your ICP criteria in HubSpot. If the company already exists as a contact or deal, the signal is added as an activity. If not, a new record is created with the signal as the source.
  • Slack alert: Routes to your qualifying SDR with company name, signal type, why it matches ICP, and a suggested opening line
Why it matters to 2026

Your MQL → SQL target is 40% and SDR utilisation ≥ 80%. Both are easier to hit when the qualifying SDR is working a ranked queue of warm signals rather than a flat list of cold contacts. Signal-based outbound consistently outperforms cold by 2–3× in reply rate.

The Nordic registry angle — Proff.no, Proff.se, Finder.fi — is particularly valuable in Scandinavia because company financials and employee counts are public. A company that filed 40% revenue growth last year and has 45 employees is a perfect Huma ICP. That data exists, it just needs to be surfaced automatically.

Integrating with the existing AI prospecting tool is the key adoption unlock. If signals land in the tool SDRs already use every day, the behaviour change required is zero. No new tab, no new inbox — just better leads in the same queue.
